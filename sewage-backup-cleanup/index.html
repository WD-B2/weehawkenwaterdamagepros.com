A sewage backup is an emergency that calls for caution before speed. The water is grossly contaminated, and it can spread across a basement floor and into walls in minutes. The 24/7 line is answered at night and on holidays, and the first rule is to keep people and pets out of the affected area.

Sewage backup: what to do first

Safety steps

  • Stop using sinks, toilets, showers and washers so you do not add more water to the system.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with a weakened immune system out of the area.
  • Do not touch wet outlets or appliances. If water is near the panel, call the utility or 911 first.
  • Do not try to clean it with towels or a household vacuum. That spreads contamination.
  • Photograph the damage and call the line. If the backup seems to be in the street or the building's main line, tell your building manager and your sewer authority.

Why sewage is handled differently

Restoration standards call this Category 3 water, meaning it is grossly contaminated and may carry harmful microorganisms. Porous materials that touched it, including carpet, pad, insulation and drywall, are usually removed rather than dried. Hard surfaces can be cleaned and disinfected, and crews wear protective equipment such as respirators, gloves and boots.

Callers often describe a downstairs drain that backed up with raw sewage, and that is exactly the situation this page covers. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard guides how restoration professionals classify and handle it, and you can ask any company how it protects occupants and prevents cross-contamination.

A plain-language look at the symptoms

Many people first notice a gurgling drain, a bad smell near a floor drain, water coming up in a basement tub or toilet that will not flush, or a damp spot around the drain. These are signs the line is blocked or overwhelmed. The trade term for the cleanup is Category 3 extraction, but what you need to know is that a backup is not a normal spill, and safe removal comes before anything else.

How a sewage cleanup is done

  1. Contain and protect

    The area is isolated, and crews put on protective gear. Foot traffic is limited to prevent spreading contamination.

  2. Extract and remove

    Contaminated water is pumped out. Affected carpet, pad, baseboards and low drywall are removed and bagged.

  3. Clean and sanitize

    Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products, and drying equipment is set.

  4. Verify and rebuild

    Moisture readings are taken and removed materials are replaced after the space is dry.

Weehawken's combined sewer system

Weehawken's sewage is handled by the North Hudson Sewerage Authority, which also serves Hoboken, West New York and Union City and describes 107 miles of combined sewers that carry sewage and stormwater in the same pipes. Combined systems can surcharge when heavy rain overloads them, which is how a storm turns into a basement backup.

The Authority's Boulevard East project lists frequent flooding of streets and basements in Weehawken as a core reason for upgrading pipes, manholes and a pump station. If your backup happened during a storm, report it to the Authority, because that information helps them understand where the system is struggling. Questions people ask

Do you handle sewage backup emergencies at night or on weekends in Weehawken?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. Crews are sent the same day when available. While you wait, stop using water, keep everyone out of the area and avoid touching anything that is wet.

Can I clean up sewage myself?

Small, clearly limited spills in a bathroom can be cleaned with protective gear. A backup into a basement or across a floor is best handled by professionals because of contamination and hidden spread into materials.

Who fixes the blocked line?

A licensed plumber clears the line, and if the main is in the street, your sewer authority may need to be notified. Restoration follows.

Will insurance cover it?

Only if your policy includes sewer or water backup coverage. Standard homeowners policies often exclude it. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

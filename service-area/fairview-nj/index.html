Six emergency touchpoints for a Fairview property

These are the points where a few minutes of preparation pay off. Walk through them now and keep the list handy, because a wet floor is a bad moment to start looking for a shutoff.

  1. Know the main water shutoff

    Find it in the basement, utility room or meter closet. In a multi-unit building, ask the owner or super which valve serves your unit.

  2. Locate the electrical panel

    If water reaches outlets or the panel, leave the breaker alone and stay out of the wet area. Call the line and an electrician.

  3. Photograph before cleanup

    Take photos and short video of the damage, the source and any affected belongings. Insurers ask for these.

  4. Move small valuables up

    Lift papers, electronics and rugs onto dry surfaces. Leave heavy furniture alone until the crew arrives.

  5. Tell the building manager

    In a condo or rental, the association or landlord may need to shut off a riser or open a neighboring unit.

  6. Save the damaged material

    Keep wet carpet, pad or drywall pieces until your insurer says otherwise. They show how far the water went.

Fairview geography and why it matters for water

The borough takes its name from its view of the Hackensack River valley, and Fairview Cemetery and Mount Moriah Cemetery run along the western slope of the Hudson Palisades. If your property sits on a slope, check where your gutters and downspouts discharge during heavy rain, because runoff that is directed at a foundation can find its way into a lower level.

Fairview borders Cliffside Park and Ridgefield in Bergen County and North Bergen in Hudson County. U.S. Route 1/9, Route 63 and County Route 501 all pass through the borough. For flood zone questions on a specific address, check the FEMA flood map, and ask the borough building department about local permit rules.

How scope and price are handled

We explain the scope and the price before work starts. That means a plain description of what will be extracted, what will be dried and what, if anything, has to be removed. The standard behind the work

Our work follows the published industry reference, ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration. It asks crews to identify the water source and category, set drying goals and record readings. For a Fairview property, that means the paperwork you receive explains what was wet, what was dried and what was removed.

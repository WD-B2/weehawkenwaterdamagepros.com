Emergency water damage restoration in Ridgefield, NJ

Ridgefield was incorporated on May 26, 1892, from portions of Ridgefield Township, and it covers 2.87 square miles, of which 0.33 square miles is water. At the 2020 census it had 11,501 residents and 4,237 housing units. That mix of homes, small shops and low-lying ground makes basement and storm water calls common here.

Our crews serve Ridgefield ZIP 07657 around the clock. When you call, we ask where the water is, whether it is still running and what it has reached. Then we explain the scope and the price before any work begins. If the water is from a sewer line, tell us right away so the crew arrives prepared.

Why Ridgefield properties see basement and sewer trouble

The borough sits at an elevation of about 10 feet and is described as part of the Hackensack River floodplain. Parts of the low-lying ground, including Skeetkill Creek Marsh, are set aside as nature reserves, and Overpeck Creek lies to the north. Low ground tends to stay wet longer after heavy rain, which puts pressure on basements and drain lines.

For a specific address, check the FEMA flood map to see whether it falls in a mapped flood zone. Ask the Ridgefield building department about permits, and ask your water and sewer provider about service outside your building.

Six emergency touchpoints for a Ridgefield property

  1. Check the main shutoff

    Know where the main valve is before you need it. Most homes have it near the meter or the front of the basement.

  2. Watch the floor drains

    If a basement drain backs up, stop using sinks, tubs and washers and call. Do not run the dishwasher or flush.

  3. Stay off wet electrics

    Do not step into standing water near the panel or outlets. Call the line and an electrician.

  4. Photograph the water line

    Take photos of the water level on walls and furniture. The mark shows how high the water reached.

  5. Raise what you can

    Lift boxes, rugs and electronics onto dry surfaces. Leave heavy items for the crew.

  6. Keep your insurer's number handy

    Report the loss promptly. Ask what they need from the restoration crew, such as photos and readings.

Dealing with sewage safely

Sewage backup needs extra care. Keep children and pets out, avoid contact with the water and do not try to clean it with household tools. The crew uses protective gear and removes what cannot be cleaned. Call the line and say it is sewage so we send the right equipment.

Scope and price

We explain the scope and the price before work starts. If moisture readings change the plan, we tell you before the work changes. Ask your insurer about coverage, and we can supply photos and readings to support your claim.

The standard behind the work

For sewage and floodwater, the published standard ANSI/IICRC S500 treats the water as highly contaminated, so affected porous materials are usually removed rather than cleaned. We explain that before the work starts so Ridgefield owners understand why carpet, pad and lower drywall may come out.

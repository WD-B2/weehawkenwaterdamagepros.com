An appliance leak turns urgent as soon as water leaves the machine. A failed washer valve can run for an hour before anyone notices, and a dishwasher or ice maker line can leak behind cabinets for days. The 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, and the first step is to close the appliance's own shutoff.

In condo and rental buildings, such as those covered on the Port Imperial page, an appliance leak can reach neighboring units.

If an appliance is leaking right now

Immediate steps

  • Close the shutoff valve behind or beside the appliance. If you cannot find it, close the main.
  • Unplug the appliance only if the plug is dry and you are standing on a dry surface. Otherwise turn off its circuit at the panel if the panel is dry.
  • Move the appliance carefully if safe, to see where the water is coming from.
  • Soak up standing water with towels and photograph the machine, the hose and the floor.
  • Call the line and tell us whether the floor is wood, tile or carpet.

Which appliances leak, and how

Washing machines usually leak from an inlet hose, a loose clamp or a failed valve. Dishwashers leak at the door gasket, supply connector or drain hose. Refrigerators leak from the ice maker line or defrost drain. The water is usually clean supply water, which is good news, but long exposure can still damage subfloors and cabinets.

A washer valve that breaks and runs undetected for even an hour can put water through a floor to the unit below. If you live in a multi-unit building, tell the neighbors beneath you and your building manager right away.

How cleanup works

  1. Remove the water

    Wet vacuums and extraction units clear the floor and any wet padding.

  2. Check hidden areas

    Moisture meters and thermal imaging check toe kicks, cabinet bases, walls and the floor below.

  3. Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the space, and wet flooring is evaluated for whether it can stay.

  4. Repair

    Once readings are normal, flooring, baseboards and cabinets are repaired or replaced.

For water that has gone into cabinets and under sinks, the kitchen water damage page covers the cabinet side. If wood flooring is cupping or lifting, see hardwood floor water damage for what can be saved.

Appliance leaks in Weehawken homes

Weehawken has many apartments and condos where washers and dishwashers share walls and ceilings with neighbors. A small leak in one unit can reach several, which is why building rules about hoses and shutoffs matter. Braided stainless hoses and a washing machine drip pan with a drain are common precautions, and your building manager can tell you what the rules are.

In older houses, laundry hookups are often in basements next to floor drains. That helps with a small leak but not with a long one, so the same first step applies: close the valve and call.

Hoses, valves and simple prevention

Rubber washer hoses age and bulge near the connector. Braided stainless hoses last longer, and a shutoff that closes when the machine is idle limits damage if one fails. Dishwasher supply lines and ice maker tubing deserve a glance whenever the appliance is pulled out. These steps cost little compared with a soaked floor and a neighbor's ceiling.

What to do before the crew arrives

Safety steps for an appliance leak

  • Unplug the appliance only if the cord and the floor around it are dry.
  • Close the supply valve behind or beside the appliance.
  • Stay off wet floors near outlets, power strips and the panel.
  • Do not run a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Photograph the leak and the floor before wiping anything.
  • Call the line and tell us what the appliance is.

The published standard for professional work, ANSI/IICRC S500, treats water from a supply line or a clean appliance feed as the least contaminated category, but it can become more contaminated the longer it sits. Quick extraction keeps the job in the simpler category.

Questions people ask

Do you handle appliance leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Weehawken?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day. Crews are sent the same day when available, and while you wait you should close the appliance valve and photograph the machine, hose and floor.

Can a washer leak damage the unit below me?

Yes. Water goes through the floor system into the ceiling below. Tell your building manager and the neighbor below right away, and document your unit before anything is moved.

Should I keep the broken hose?

Yes, if you can. It helps your insurer and sometimes the manufacturer understand what failed.

Does insurance pay for appliance water damage?

Sudden failures are often covered for the water damage, though not always the appliance. Gradual leaks are usually excluded, so ask your insurer what applies.

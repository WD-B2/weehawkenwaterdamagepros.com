Other nearby towns

Calls also come from nearby towns without their own pages, including Edgewater, Moonachie, Little Ferry, Palisades Park, Union City, West New York, Hoboken, North Bergen, Secaucus, Cliffside Park and Kearny. If you live in one of them, call the line and give your street address. We will confirm coverage and explain how soon a crew can arrive.

Wherever you are, the same published standard applies. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference for how water is classified and how drying is documented, and it guides our work in every town on this page.

Availability depends on where crews are at the moment. Same-day response is offered when available, and we will say plainly if timing is tight.

What changes from town to town

Each town has its own utilities, building rules and housing mix. Jersey City is a large city with many towers, brownstones and two-family houses. Fairview and Ridgefield are Bergen County boroughs with older houses, small apartment buildings and commercial strips along the main roads. A crew plans differently for each, and the town pages explain how.

Hudson County and Bergen County also run separate offices for permits and records, which matters if repairs need approval. When you call, give the town and street address so we can plan for access, parking and any building rules before the crew arrives.

How to check your own risk

Use the FEMA flood map to see whether your address falls in a mapped flood zone. Ask your town's building department about permits for repairs and ask your water or sewer provider about problems outside your building. These sources are specific to your address, which is why we point you to them instead of guessing.

Before you call: three quick checks

First, confirm the water is not near electricity. Second, find the shutoff for the fixture or the main and close it if you can reach it safely. Third, take a few photos from the doorway before moving anything. These steps take two minutes and make the call faster, because we can focus on what the crew needs to bring.

If you cannot find the shutoff, describe the building when you call and we will help you think through where it might be. In multi-unit buildings, the valve for a unit is often in a closet, under a sink or in a utility room near the meter.

Getting a crew to your door

Travel time depends on traffic, the time of day and where crews are working. The Lincoln Tunnel approach, the Route 495 corridor and the Hudson waterfront can all slow movement, so we ask for the street address first and give you an honest estimate. Same-day response is offered when available, and we will not promise what we cannot deliver.

Once on site, the first jobs are to stop the water, protect what can be protected and begin extraction. Drying follows and readings are logged. For services, start at the services page. For our address and phone details, see the contact page.

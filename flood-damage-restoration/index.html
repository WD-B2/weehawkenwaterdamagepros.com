Flooding is an emergency that mixes risk with speed. Water from rivers, storm surge or overloaded drains can carry sewage and chemicals, and it can reach outlets and appliances. The 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, and the first step is to stay out of the water and protect people.

When flood water is an emergency

Any water that entered from outside the building, or that rose through a floor drain, is flood water for restoration purposes. Treat it as contaminated, keep children and pets away, and do not run electronics in the affected area. If water is near the panel, a meter or gas appliances, call the utility or 911 before calling us.

Safe steps before the crew arrives

  • Do not walk through standing water if outlets, cords or appliances are in it.
  • Turn off power only if the panel is dry and reachable without entering the water.
  • Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, and wash hands thoroughly afterward.
  • Photograph the waterline and damaged belongings for your claim.
  • Move valuables and documents up to a dry level.

How flood damage differs from a plumbing leak

A broken supply line releases clean water, which is easier to dry and clean. Flood water is different because of where it has been. It can leave silt and residue, and porous materials such as carpet, insulation and unfinished drywall that sat in it are often removed rather than dried. Hard materials such as tile and sealed concrete can usually be cleaned and sanitized.

Flood risk around Weehawken

Weehawken sits on the Hudson waterfront and the Palisades, so flood exposure depends heavily on the address. A New Jersey DEP study of the Hudson waterfront names Weehawken Cove as one of three entry points for Hurricane Sandy floodwater in the Hoboken area, and notes that some floodwater also moved from Hoboken into Weehawken.

For your exact address, check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask the township building department about local flood rules before rebuilding. The North Hudson Sewerage Authority has also listed frequent flooding of streets and basements in Weehawken as a reason for its Boulevard East infrastructure project, so a rain-driven flood can come through the sewer connection as well as over the street.

Flood insurance is its own policy

The New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flooding, including flooding that accompanies a hurricane. Flood coverage needs a separate policy, and it normally carries a 30 day waiting period before it begins. If you have flood coverage, report the loss at once. If you do not, ask your insurer what is and is not covered, because sewer backup is sometimes handled by a separate endorsement.

Document everything: the waterline, each room, belongings, and any receipts for lodging or repairs. The same state guidance tells owners to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, which is where prompt water removal and drying fit.

Questions people ask

Do you handle flood emergencies at night or on weekends in Weehawken?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24 hours a day, every day. Crews are sent the same day when available, and the best thing you can do while waiting is stay out of the water, keep power off in wet areas where it is safe to do so and take photos.

Is flood water always contaminated?

Restoration practice treats flood water as contaminated because it can carry sewage, chemicals and debris from outside the building. That is why porous materials that sat in it are often removed.

Does homeowners insurance cover flooding?

Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flooding. Flood coverage is a separate policy, often with a waiting period, so check what you carry and report the loss right away.

How do I check my flood zone?

The FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you search by address. Your township building department can tell you how the map affects rebuilding at your address.
